# Broiler Litter Management Guidelines

Litter quality directly influences air quality, footpad health, and disease levels.

Litter Maintenance Protocols:

1. Ideal Litter Moisture:
- Keep litter moisture between 20% and 25%.
- Test: Squeeze a handful of litter. It should form a loose ball that crumbles easily when released. If it sticks together in a solid ball, it is too wet (above 30%). If it does not form a ball, it is too dry.

2. Wet Litter Problems:
- Wet litter causes bacterial multiplication, increases ammonia gas release, and facilitates the sporulation of coccidia parasites (leading to coccidiosis).
- It also causes footpad dermatitis and breast blisters in broilers.

3. Managing Wet Spots:
- Regularly inspect under drinkers. If water lines are leaking or pressure is too high, adjust the regulators.
- Manually remove heavily caked, wet litter and replace it with clean, dry wood shavings or chopped straw.
- Turn the litter regularly to keep it loose and promote drying. Add litter conditioners (e.g. acidifying agents or clay absorbents) to neutralize ammonia release.
