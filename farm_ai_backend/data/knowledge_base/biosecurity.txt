# Poultry Farm Biosecurity Guidelines

Biosecurity is the most effective method of preventing the introduction and spread of disease in a poultry farm. Adhering to strict biosecurity protocols protects the flock and ensures commercial viability.

Key biosecurity protocols:
1. Access Control: Limit farm visitors. Keep gates locked and post signs indicating restricted access. All visitors must log their names, purposes, and contact details before entering.
2. Protective Wear: Anyone entering the poultry sheds must wear clean boots, coveralls, and hairnets. Disinfectant footbaths must be installed at the entrance of each shed. Refresh the disinfectant in the footbaths daily.
3. Equipment Sanitation: Clean and disinfect all tools, crates, and machinery before introducing them into the production zone. Never share equipment with neighboring farms.
4. Pest and Wild Bird Control: Seal all openings to prevent wild birds, rodents, and insects from entering. Wild birds can carry Newcastle disease, infectious bronchitis, and avian influenza.
5. Litter Management: Clean up feed spills immediately to avoid attracting wild rodents and birds.
6. Cleaning and Disinfection Cycles: Between bird batches, execute a complete clean-out. Remove all litter, wash the walls and floors with high-pressure water, and apply an approved disinfectant. Allow the shed to dry completely (down time) for at least 14 days before introducing the next batch.
