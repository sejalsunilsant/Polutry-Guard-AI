# Poultry Disease Identification and Treatment Guide

Identifying and treating diseases early prevents high mortality rates in broilers.

Common Broiler Diseases:

1. Infectious Bronchitis (IB)
- Description: A highly contagious viral respiratory disease that affects chickens of all ages.
- Symptoms: Sneezing, coughing (snick), gasping for air, nasal discharge, wet eyes, and lethargy.
- Prevention: Vaccination is the primary defense. Administer the Newcastle-Bronchitis (e.g. LaSota) booster via water lines or aerosol spray.
- Treatment: No specific antiviral treatment. Support the flock by increasing exhaust fan speeds to lower dust and ammonia, and adding electrolytes/vitamins to drinking water.

2. Coccidiosis
- Description: A parasitic disease of the intestinal tract caused by microscopic coccidia parasites.
- Symptoms: Pale combs, bloody droppings, ruffled feathers, huddled birds, decreased feed consumption, and slow weight growth.
- Prevention: Keep the litter dry. Coccidia thrive in wet, warm environments. Use coccidiostats in feed.
- Treatment: Add Amprolium to the drinking water lines immediately for 5-7 days. Clean out wet spots in litter.

3. Newcastle Disease (ND)
- Description: An acute, highly contagious viral disease.
- Symptoms: Respiratory snicks, gasping, nasal discharge, followed by nervous signs such as twisting of the head and neck (torticollis), greenish watery diarrhea, and rapid mortality.
- Prevention: Rigorous vaccination program and strict biosecurity.
- Treatment: No treatment exists. Infected flocks must be quarantined and euthanized if required by local authorities.

4. Sudden Death Syndrome (SDS)
- Description: Also known as flip-over disease, affecting fast-growing, healthy male broilers.
- Symptoms: Healthy birds suddenly thrash, flip on their backs, and die.
- Prevention: Slow down early growth rates by using lighting programs or restricting feed intake slightly during the first two weeks.
